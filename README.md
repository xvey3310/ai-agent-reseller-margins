# ai agent reseller program: how to white-label, re-bill, and price AI agents you sell to clients

Most people searching for an AI agent reseller program are not shopping for software. They are trying to answer a commercial question: can I put my logo on somebody else's AI, charge my clients more than it costs me, and keep the difference every month?

That question has a different answer depending on which of four business models you are actually signing up for, and vendors use "reseller program" loosely enough that the word alone tells you nothing. So before comparing CloseBot against anything else, get the model straight. Then look at the numbers, because the margin lives in three places: what you pay wholesale, what you can re-bill, and whether the vendor can reprice you mid-contract.

## The four deals hiding behind the phrase "reseller program"

Centricall published a breakdown in August 2026 that sorts these arrangements honestly, and the distinction matters more than any feature list.

| Model | What you pay | What you earn | What you control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Referral / affiliate | Nothing | Commission on client spend, commonly 15–30% | Nothing: not price, product, or renewal |
| Reseller with sub-accounts | Per-seat or per-client fee | The spread above your cost | Pricing within vendor limits |
| White label subscription | Flat monthly platform fee | The full spread above wholesale | Branding and retail price, not the rate card |
| Perpetual licence | One-time payment | Everything above your own hosting costs | All of it |

If your goal is recurring revenue under your own brand with clients who renew with you rather than with the vendor, you are looking at the white-label row. Referral programs pay you once for an introduction and then keep the customer. That is a fine business, but it is not the one most people imagine when they type "ai agent reseller program" into a search box.

CloseBot's agency motion sits squarely in the white-label row, with one twist worth understanding: instead of a flat markup on a licence fee, it meters usage and lets you set your own retail price on top. That changes both your margin and your risk.

## The margin math, with the numbers you can actually verify

This is where most reseller content gets vague, so here are CloseBot's published wholesale rates:

- **Messages:** $0.012 per message on the agency plan, fully re-billable.
- **User seats:** $5.00 per seat per month, billed hourly at $0.0069, re-billable.
- **Storage:** $0.006 per MB per day, re-billable.
- **Token costs:** tracked for you and optionally re-billable, either on their own or combined with message markup.

A client doing 2,000 AI messages a month costs you $24 in wholesale message fees. Bill it at $0.03 and that line becomes $60. Add one client seat at $5 cost, billed at $25, and you are at roughly $80 of gross margin on a single account before any platform subscription of your own.

CloseBot's own plans page runs a calculator based on polled agencies and states an average of $500 billed per client per month, with a comparison putting CloseBot agencies at 5x to 6.3x what agencies charge on cheaper alternatives. Treat those as vendor figures, because that is exactly what they are. The verifiable part is the wholesale rate card above, and that is enough to build your own model.

The same page quotes the spread of what agencies actually charge: "some agencies charge as little as $100/month while others make $10k+ monthly from a single client." That is a wide range because it reflects two different products. A $100/month client is buying a chatbot. A $10k/month client is buying booked appointments in an expensive vertical, and the pricing conversation starts from the value of a closed deal.

## What it costs you to resell, all in

CloseBot is not the whole bill, and the reseller math in most guides quietly ignores that.

Because CloseBot is CRM-native rather than channel-native, an agent answers the conversations sitting in your CRM inbox. The tool does not connect to Instagram or WhatsApp itself. If a client already runs GoHighLevel with those channels wired into the Conversations inbox, CloseBot can reply there. If the client has no CRM at all, you are selling them a CRM first, then layering CloseBot on top. A third-party review published in August 2026 put a realistic solo set-up at roughly $181 a month, combining CloseBot's entry business tier with a GoHighLevel Starter plan.

Two more cost wildcards to model before you quote a client:

**Agent Node billing.** CloseBot bills one message as one segment, except when you unlock the Agent Node's "unlimited potential" (many tools, unlimited instruction length). Then billing moves to token costs and a single message can consume several segments. Heavy, tool-rich agents cost more than the headline rate suggests.

**AI provider keys.** CloseBot's public plans FAQ states plainly that the platform does not allow bring-your-own-key, described as a security decision. Older V2 help documentation says the opposite, stating that V2 requires you to use your own API keys. The two pages contradict each other, so ask support directly before you build a client budget around either answer.

## Six questions that decide whether you keep the margin

Centricall's August 2026 piece makes the point that headline margin percentages in this category are mostly marketing, and that the contract terms decide the outcome. Applied to any AI agent reseller program, including this one, the short list is:

1. **Rebilling rights.** Can you bill through your own payment processor at a markup, or only at cost? CloseBot's agency plan explicitly supports markup on messages, seats, storage, and tokens, with Stripe Connect handling the flow.
2. **Price-lock and notice.** What protection do you have if the vendor restructures agency tiers? CloseBot publishes month-to-month terms with no contract, which cuts both ways: easy to leave, and no protection if wholesale rates change.
3. **Data portability.** If you leave, how do you extract transcripts, recordings, and client configuration? Ask before your client book depends on the answer.
4. **Compliance cost.** Is HIPAA included, or billed as an upgrade? On CloseBot it is a Growth-tier feature, so if you are quoting a clinic, that quote needs the custom tier in it.
5. **Minimums and exclusivity.** Are you committing to volume you have not sold yet? CloseBot has no revenue share or minimum, which is unusual and working in your favour.
6. **Vendor risk.** The cautionary example in that same piece: Synthflow now publishes only an enterprise plan with a $30,000 annual minimum, having dropped the self-serve tiers agencies built businesses on. Anyone who priced a client book around the old tiers had to requote or rebuild every agent.

Demand for this category is not the problem. Grand View Research valued conversational AI at $11.58 billion in 2024 and forecasts $41.39 billion by 2030, and a Deepgram and Opus Research survey found 84% of organisations increasing voice AI budgets. The same survey found only 21% were very satisfied with the agents they already run, which is precisely the gap a competent reseller gets paid to close.

## Where CloseBot is the wrong pick

Text only. CloseBot handles SMS, website chat, and email. It does not do voice calls or video. If your offer is an AI receptionist answering the phone, this is not the platform for it.

It depends on a CRM in practice. If you want to sell AI agents to businesses with no CRM and no intention of buying one, you are fighting the architecture.

No refunds. CloseBot states this outright. What you get instead is a free-forever plan under 100 messages a month plus a 7-day trial of any paid plan, and the trial is where your testing belongs.

Documentation is thinner than the marketing. Fin's comparison page notes that CloseBot does not publicly document SOC 2 or ISO certifications, with HIPAA restricted to its top tier. If your prospects send security questionnaires, that gap will surface in procurement, and you should know it before it does. Reviews of the platform also flag a genuine learning curve: the job flow builder is not a ten-minute setup, and the quality of the conversations tracks how much configuration work you put in.

On the other side, CloseBot's own V2 announcement claims 14 G2 awards, and the platform's published numbers include over 1 million booked appointments, roughly 150,000 messages a day, and more than 1,000 agencies. Those are vendor-reported, not audited. The 1,000-agency figure is at least a directional signal that the reseller model has been running in production rather than in a launch post.

## FAQ

**What is an AI agent reseller program?**
An arrangement that lets you sell another company's AI agents to your own clients. It covers at least four different deals: referral commission, reseller with sub-accounts, white label, and perpetual licence. Establish which one you are being offered before you compare pricing, because they pay out completely differently.

**How much does CloseBot's reseller plan cost?**
$397 per month on the agency track, flat, with unlimited agents and white-label rebilling. Messages are billed at $0.012 each and are re-billable, user seats at $5 per seat per month, and storage at $0.006 per MB per day, all of which you can mark up. The business track starts at $64 per month and does not include white labelling or rebilling.

**Can I charge whatever I want my clients?**
On usage, yes. You set the markup on messages, seats, storage, and tokens, per client if you want. What the platform does not do is give you the underlying wholesale rate card as your price list, so your retail pricing is your decision and your risk.

**Does CloseBot do voice agents?**
No. It is a text platform covering SMS, website chat, and email inside your CRM. Voice resellers need a different product, and the reseller economics in voice are structured differently, with wholesale minutes rather than per-message rates.

**Is there a free way to test the reseller model?**
Yes, two. There is a free-forever plan capped at 100 messages a month, and a 7-day trial of any paid plan before billing starts. There are no refunds after that, so use the trial period for the testing you would otherwise want to do later.
